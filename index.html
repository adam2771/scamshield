Message safety check

Paste an SMS, email or WhatsApp message. Remove names, account numbers and other private details first.

Common red flags

Unexpected prize, refund or money offer.
Threats about account closure, arrest or urgent fees.
Requests for OTPs, PINs, passwords or card details.
Unexpected links or attachments.
Pressure to keep the conversation secret.
A low-risk result does not mean a message is genuine. Scams can be carefully written and may not trigger any simple keyword rule.

Website and link check

Examines the address itself. It does not open the link or send it anywhere.

Check the real domain

Read the domain from right to left: the registered-looking name is immediately before the ending such as .co.za.
Look for misspellings, extra hyphens and swapped letters.
HTTPS is encryption, not proof that a site is honest.
Shortened links hide the final destination — don't trust them just because the preview looks familiar.
Never paste password-reset links, private invitation links or URLs containing personal tokens into a checker.

If you think you've been scammed

Work through this checklist. If money or banking access may be at risk, contact your bank immediately through its official app or a trusted number.

Avoid recovery scams

Don't pay an upfront fee to someone promising to recover lost money.
Don't install remote-access software because an unexpected caller tells you to.
Don't share OTPs, PINs or passwords with anyone who contacts you.
Don't delete evidence before saving it.
South Africa: For bank fraud, contact your bank's official fraud team first. Use official government or police channels for further reporting; don't rely on phone numbers sent to you by a suspected scammer.

Everyday protection

Calls: Let unknown callers leave a message. Call organisations back using a number you find independently.
SMS: Don't follow unexpected payment, delivery or account links.
WhatsApp: Be cautious of urgent requests from familiar names; verify with the person through another channel.
Websites: Check the exact domain before entering details.
Payments: Verify account details and unusual requests with someone you trust before paying.

Free phone security setup

Keep Android, your browser and apps updated.
Enable your phone's built-in caller ID and spam protection if available.
Use the browser's built-in Safe Browsing feature.
Install apps from trusted app stores and check the developer.
Review app permissions; remove access an app doesn't need.
Use unique passwords and two-step verification.
